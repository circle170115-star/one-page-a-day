Parenting diary · 01

Early morning, before anyone else is awake

Wednesday, 11 November 2026 · 3 min

At six the living room is quiet the way water is quiet. I leave the window half open and cold air crosses my ankles on its way in. The child is still asleep; I hold the cup in hands that are not quite awake. For this hour it feels as though I am the only person in the world who is up, and strangely, that is what settles me.

Lately my child opens their eyes and asks, before anything else, “What are we doing today?” If I don't have an answer ready, they simply ask again. The urgency of it tires me, and at the same time I am grateful: it means my day now contains a question.

Yesterday I answered, “Nothing.” They paused, then said, “Then let's do nothing together.” And lay face down on the floor, looking out of the window. I lay down beside them. Ten minutes, I think. I don't remember what we looked at. I only remember the feeling that those ten minutes would stay with me a long time.

What raising a child has taught me is that memory does not keep events; it keeps temperatures. I forget what happened on a given day, but I remember for years whether the back of my child's hand was warm or cool. So I write now instead of taking photographs. A photograph keeps the smile; writing can keep the feeling just before it.

Once a week I pick up the brush. I don't try to write well. I draw a line, stop, draw it again. The best thing about learning calligraphy is that a wrong stroke stays where it is. Ink cannot be erased, so the place where I slipped becomes part of the character. Raising a child is a little like that.

When my child wakes today they will ask again. This time I plan to say it first: let's do nothing together.

— from the living room, early morning
